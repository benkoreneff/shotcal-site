ShotCal

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 10 November 2026

ShotCal is a personal macOS menu bar app that turns a screenshot into a Google Calendar event. It runs on your Mac. There is no ShotCal server, and the developer collects, stores and shares no data about you.

Screenshots

When you press the shortcut and select part of your screen, ShotCal sends that image to the Anthropic API, using your own Anthropic API key, to read the event details in it. The image goes from your Mac straight to Anthropic. ShotCal deletes its temporary copy of the image as soon as the capture has been processed. Anthropic handles the image under its own terms and privacy policy.

Google Calendar

If you connect a Google account, ShotCal asks for one permission: to view and edit events on your calendars (https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.events). ShotCal uses it only to:

  • create the events you capture, and
  • delete an event that ShotCal created, when you choose to undo it.

ShotCal does not read, list, download or change your other events. Event details go from your Mac straight to Google, which handles them under its privacy policy.

ShotCal's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Data from Google is not transferred to anyone, is not used for advertising, and is not used to train AI models.

What is stored on your Mac

  • In the macOS Keychain: your Anthropic API key, the Google OAuth client ID and client secret you entered, and the Google sign-in token.
  • In the app's preferences: plain settings such as the model ID, the calendar ID and the keyboard shortcut.
  • In the macOS system log: short diagnostic entries, for example that a capture ran or an error message.

None of this leaves your Mac, except that the keys and token are sent to Anthropic and Google respectively to authenticate your own requests.

What the developer collects

Nothing. ShotCal has no analytics, no telemetry, no accounts and no servers. The developer never receives your screenshots, your calendar data, your keys or your tokens.

Removing access and data

  • Choose Disconnect in ShotCal's settings to delete the Google sign-in token from your Mac and ask Google to revoke it.
  • You can also remove ShotCal's access at any time at myaccount.google.com/connections.
  • To remove everything else, delete the ShotCal items in Keychain Access and delete the app.
